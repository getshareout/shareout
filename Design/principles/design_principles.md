# Design Principles

## Core Principle: Effortless Clarity

> **"If a grandma can't figure it out in 3 seconds, we failed."**

ShareOut exists to make publishing instant and joyful. Every design decision filters through one question: **Does this make it easier?**

When in doubt: **Would this feel good to someone who's never used software like this before?**

---

## The ShareOut Aesthetic

### Warm, Not Clinical

We reject the cold, sterile aesthetic of most software. Our palette leans warm — cream backgrounds, soft shadows, colors that feel like paper and ink rather than screens and pixels.

### Confident, Not Loud

Good design doesn't shout. A single blue button on a quiet canvas says more than a rainbow of gradients. We use color sparingly and purposefully.

### Friendly, Not Childish

Rounded corners. Generous spacing. Soft edges. But never cartoonish. We're approachable without being unserious.

### Modern, Not Trendy

We don't chase trends. No purple gradients because AI tools use them. No cold blue-gray glass because it's hot. We make choices that will age well.

**Liquid glass is the exception we embrace** — but as *structure*, not decoration. One unified warm canvas, with translucent surfaces floating on top to build depth and hierarchy. Warm-tinted, high-contrast text, always in service of clarity. Never cold, never blue-gray, never glass-for-its-own-sake.

## Design Pillars

### 1. One Thing at a Time

Every screen has ONE primary action. Everything else is secondary or hidden.

```
BAD:  "Publish" "Save Draft" "Preview" "Share" "Settings" all screaming equally
GOOD: Big friendly "Publish" button. Everything else tucked away.
```

**Rule:** If you can't point to THE button, redesign.

Also: one focal point · one story per screen · one decision at a time.

### 2. Show, Don't Decorate

Every element earns its place. No decorative flourishes. No visual noise. If it doesn't help the user accomplish something, it doesn't exist.

### 3. Instant Feedback

Users should never wonder "did it work?"

- Actions complete visibly
- States change immediately
- Progress is always clear
- Success feels like a small celebration

**Speed is a feature.** Fast interfaces feel good. Slow is broken.

### 4. Words First

Icons are shortcuts, not replacements. Important actions get labels. We write like humans talk, not like software manuals.

### 5. Generous Spacing

Cramped = confusing. We breathe.

- Touch targets: 44px minimum (thumbs exist)
- Whitespace is a feature, not waste
- Group related things, separate unrelated
- When in doubt: add more space

---

## Layout Philosophy

The best interface is obvious.

One primary action · One focal point · One story per screen · One decision at a time.

---

## The Grandma Test

Our north star in practice:

- No jargon ("Deploy artifact" → "Share your page")
- No ambiguity (one obvious next step)
- No hunting (important things are visible)
- No mystery (clear feedback for every action)

---

## Summary

ShareOut design is:

- **Easy** — One clear path, always
- **Warm** — Human, not robotic
- **Quiet** — Confident, not loud
- **Fast** — Responsive, not sluggish
- **Inclusive** — Works for everyone
- **Distinctive** — Own personality, never generic

---

## Design North Star

Ideas deserve to exist. ShareOut makes that possible.

Every design decision should reinforce this belief.

If a design makes creation feel harder, it is wrong.

If a design makes publishing feel intimidating, it is wrong.

If a design makes possibility feel smaller, it is wrong.
