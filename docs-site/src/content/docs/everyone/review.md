title: Review & approvals
description: Pin comments on an artifact, resolve and assign them, see which version they were written on, and approve a version.

Comments turn an artifact into something people can review: leave a note on the exact
spot, hand it to someone, close it when it's done, and sign off on the version that's
ready.

## Pin a comment to a spot

1. Open the comments panel in the artifact viewer and choose **Pin to page**.
2. The panel closes and the artifact stays fully readable, with a pin cursor and a
   hint: *"Click where you want to leave a comment · Esc to cancel"*.
3. Click the spot. A small composer opens right there. Click somewhere else to move
   the pin; it follows the page as you scroll.
4. Write your comment and send it.

Press **Esc**, hit **Cancel**, click outside the artifact, or move to another page or
tab to stop placing the pin. If the comment can't be posted, the panel opens with your
text so nothing is lost.

## Work through comments

The **Comments** tab in the Inspector on [Home](/everyone/your-workspace/) is where
review happens:

- **Open / All** — show only unresolved threads, or everything.
- **Resolve / Reopen** — close a thread once it's handled, or bring it back.
- **Assign** — pick someone from the people on this artifact. They get a notification
  (unless you assign yourself) and the thread shows an **Assigned to** chip.
  **Unassign** clears it.
- **on vN** — a comment written on an older version than the live one shows which
  version it was on, so you know the artifact may have changed since.

## Approve a version

In **Details**, **Approve this version** records that you signed off on the live
version. **Withdraw approval** takes it back. Approvals are a record, not a gate:
publishing still works without them.

**History** shows each version's comment count and approvals, so you can see at a glance
which version was reviewed and by whom. See [Version history](/everyone/version-history/).

## Who can review

Resolving, assigning and approving need comment access to the artifact. You have it if
you are:

- the **owner**, or a **collaborator** on it (any level);
- a **member of the workspace**, when the artifact is visible to the workspace or public;
- someone it was shared with at the **Comment** level, for example a
  [client](/teams/external-sharing/).

Viewing alone is not enough: people with **View** access, or a signed-in stranger on a
public artifact, can't resolve, assign or approve.
